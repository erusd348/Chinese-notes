📝 Notes
请问 — The Polite Opener
请问 (“may I ask”) is the standard way to stop a stranger for directions. Dropping it is not wrong, but with it you will always sound courteous rather than abrupt.
请问，图书馆在哪儿？qǐng wèn， tú shū guǎn zài nǎ ér？Excuse me, where is the library?
Place Names Are Built From Blocks
Most place words are transparent once you know the parts: 图书馆 (books + building), 食堂 (meal + hall), 宿舍 (lodge + house), 书店 (books + shop), 医院 (medicine + court).
我去图书馆。wǒ qù tú shū guǎn。I am going to the library.
我去食堂。wǒ qù shí táng。I am going to the dining hall.
📚 Grammar
1. 在 — Where Something Is
Subject + 在 + Location
在 is the verb “to be at / in / on”. It always takes a place word after it, and it is the only verb you need for locations — no “is / are” change.
图书馆在那儿。tú shū guǎn zài nà ér。The library is there.
银行在那儿。yín háng zài nà ér。The bank is there.
医院在那儿。yī yuàn zài nà ér。The hospital is there.
2. 哪儿 — Asking “Where”
Subject + 在 + 哪儿
哪儿 is the question word for a place, and it sits exactly where 那儿 / 图书馆 would sit in the answer. Northern speakers add the r-sound; 哪里 means the same.
图书馆在哪儿？tú shū guǎn zài nǎ ér？Where is the library?
银行在哪儿？yín háng zài nǎ ér？Where is the bank?
医院在哪儿？yī yuàn zài nǎ ér？Where is the hospital?
3. 去 + Place — Going Somewhere
去 + Place
去 takes the place directly — Chinese needs no preposition, so there is no equivalent of “to” here.
你去哪儿？nǐ qù nǎ ér？Where are you going?
我去天安门。wǒ qù tiān ān mén。I am going to Tiananmen.
