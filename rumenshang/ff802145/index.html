💬 Dialogue
💬 D1_
你吃什么？Nǐ chī shénme?What do you eat?
我吃饺子。Wǒ chī jiǎozi.I eat dumplings.
你喝什么？Nǐ hē shénme?What do you drink?
我喝啤酒。Wǒ hē píjiǔ.I drink beer.
你买什么？Nǐ mǎi shénme?What do you buy?
我买词典。Wǒ mǎi cídiǎn.I buy a dictionary.
📝 Notes
吃 vs. 喝 — Solid vs. Liquid
吃 is for food you chew, 喝 is for anything you drink — including soup in most of China. Choosing the wrong one is a very common beginner slip.
我吃包子。wǒ chī bāo zi。I eat steamed buns.
我喝茶。wǒ hē chá。I drink tea.
我喝牛奶。wǒ hē niú nǎi。I drink milk.
Staples on a Chinese Table
米饭 (rice), 面条 (noodles), 饺子 (dumplings), 包子 (stuffed buns) and 面包 (bread) are the everyday carbohydrate choices — a Chinese meal is built around one of them rather than around the meat dish.
我吃米饭。wǒ chī mǐ fàn。I eat rice.
我吃面条。wǒ chī miàn tiáo。I eat noodles.
📚 Grammar
1. 什么 — Asking “What”
Subject + Verb + 什么
什么 sits exactly where the answer would go, so the word order is identical to the statement. You never move it to the front of the sentence.
你吃什么？nǐ chī shén me？What do you eat?
你喝什么？nǐ hē shén me？What do you drink?
你买什么？nǐ mǎi shén me？What do you buy?
2. Subject + Verb + Object — Basic Word Order
S + V + O
Chinese follows the same Subject-Verb-Object order as English. Once you know this, asking a question is just a matter of swapping the object for a question word.
我吃饺子。wǒ chī jiǎo zi。I eat dumplings.
我喝啤酒。wǒ hē pí jiǔ。I drink beer.
我买词典。wǒ mǎi cí diǎn。I buy a dictionary.
