💬 Dialogue
我要换钱。Wǒ yào huàn qián.I want to exchange money.
你要换多少钱？Nǐ yào huàn duōshǎo qián?How much do you want to exchange?
我要换一百美元。Wǒ yào huàn yìbǎi měiyuán.I want to exchange $100.

📝 Notes
Chinese Money (RMB)
The spoken units are 块 (kuài, yuan), 毛 (máo, one tenth of a kuai) and 分 (fēn, one hundredth). Prices are read straight down the digits: X块X毛X分. In writing you will see 元 / 角 instead.
三块五毛。sān kuài wǔ máo。3.50 yuan.
十九块四毛。shí jiǔ kuài sì máo。19.40 yuan.
三十块零六毛。sān shí kuài líng liù máo。30.60 yuan.
五十块。wǔ shí kuài。50 yuan.
Measure Words Come With the Noun
A number cannot touch a noun directly — it needs a measure word: 个 (general), 杯 (cup), 瓶 (bottle), 本 (book). Learn one together with each noun you meet.
两个杯子。liǎng gè bēi zi。Two cups.
两瓶啤酒。liǎng píng pí jiǔ。Two bottles of beer.
一本书。yī běn shū。One book.
📚 Grammar
1. Numbers 1–10 and Beyond
一 二 三 四 五 六 七 八 九 十
Chinese numbers are completely regular: 十一 (11), 二十 (20), 二十五 (25), 一百 (100). Once you know 1–10 you can count to 99 with no new words.
一、二、三、四、五。yī、 èr、 sān、 sì、 wǔ。One, two, three, four, five.
六、七、八、九、十。liù、 qī、 bā、 jiǔ、 shí。Six, seven, eight, nine, ten.
2. 二 (èr) vs. 两 (liǎng) — Two
两 + Measure Word + Noun
Use 二 when counting out loud and inside compound numbers (十二, 二十, 一百二). Use 两 whenever a measure word follows: 两个, 两块, 两杯.
两个杯子。liǎng gè bēi zi。Two cups.
两杯咖啡。liǎng bēi kā fēi。Two cups of coffee.
十二块钱。shí èr kuài qián。Twelve kuai.
3. 多少 — Asking “How Much / How Many”
多少 (+ Noun)
多少 asks about an amount and usually expects a larger or open-ended answer. Place it before the noun, or let it stand alone as the object.
多少钱？duō shǎo qián？How much money?
你要换多少？nǐ yào huàn duō shǎo？How much do you want to exchange?
