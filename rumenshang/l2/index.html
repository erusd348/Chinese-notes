💬 Dialogue
你好吗？Nǐ hǎo ma?How are you?
我很好。你呢？Wǒ hěn hǎo. Nǐ ne?I'm fine. And you?
我也很好。Wǒ yě hěn hǎo.I'm also fine.
你爸爸妈妈都好吗？Nǐ bàba māma dōu hǎo ma?Are your parents both well?
他们也都很好。Tāmen yě dōu hěn hǎo.They're also both fine.
你忙吗？Nǐ máng ma?Are you busy?
我不忙。Wǒ bù máng.I'm not busy.
📝 Notes
很 — Not Always “Very”
Before an adjective, 很 often works as a simple linker rather than a strong “very”: 我很好 just means “I am fine”. Use 太 when you really mean “too / excessively”.
我很好。wǒ hěn hǎo。I am fine.
他很忙。tā hěn máng。He is busy.
Family Words Encode Age and Side
Chinese has no single word for “brother” or “sister”: 哥哥 / 弟弟 are older / younger brother, 姐姐 / 妹妹 are older / younger sister. 爱人 covers spouse in general.
我哥哥很忙。wǒ gē ge hěn máng。My older brother is busy.
我妹妹也很忙。wǒ mèi mei yě hěn máng。My younger sister is busy too.
📚 Grammar
1. 吗 — Yes/No Questions
Statement + 吗
Add 吗 to the end of a statement and it becomes a yes/no question. Nothing else changes — the word order stays exactly as it was.
你好吗？nǐ hǎo ma？How are you?
你忙吗？nǐ máng ma？Are you busy?
你累吗？nǐ lèi ma？Are you tired?
你饿吗？nǐ è ma？Are you hungry?
你渴吗？nǐ kě ma？Are you thirsty?
2. 呢 — Bouncing the Question Back
Noun / Pronoun + 呢
Put 呢 after a short phrase to ask the same question back: “And you?” / “What about...?” No verb is needed — the context supplies it.
我很好。你呢？wǒ hěn hǎo。 nǐ ne？I am fine. And you?
我不忙。你呢？wǒ bù máng。 nǐ ne？I am not busy. And you?
3. 也 and 都 — Where They Sit
Subject + 也 / 都 + Verb / Adjective
也 (also) and 都 (both / all) always come AFTER the subject and BEFORE the verb or adjective. When both appear, 也 comes first.
我也很好。wǒ yě hěn hǎo。I am also fine.
他们也都很好。tā men yě dōu hěn hǎo。They are also all fine.
