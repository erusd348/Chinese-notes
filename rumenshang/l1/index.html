📝 Notes
你好 — Literally “You Good”
The everyday greeting is a person word + 好 (good), so 你好 means literally “you good”. Swap the person word and the greeting follows automatically — this is why Chinese has no separate word for “hello”.
你好！nǐ hǎo！Hello!
老师好！lǎo shī hǎo！Hello, teacher!
Polite Expressions Come in Pairs
Chinese politeness is built from fixed pairs: 谢谢 ↔ 不客气 (thanks ↔ you are welcome) and 对不起 ↔ 没关系 (sorry ↔ it is OK). Learn both halves — you will hear the reply as often as you say the first line.
谢谢！xiè xiè！Thanks!
不客气。bù kè qì。You are welcome.
对不起！duì bù qǐ！Sorry!
没关系。méi guān xì。It is OK.
📚 Grammar
1. Person or Title + 好 — Greeting Someone
Person / Title + 好
Put the person or title first, then 好. 你 → 你好 (you); 您 → 您好 (polite “you”, for elders and strangers); 你们 → 你们好 (a group); 老师 → 老师好 (a teacher).
你好！nǐ hǎo！Hello!
您好！nín hǎo！Hello! (polite).
你们好！nǐ men hǎo！Hello everyone!
老师好！lǎo shī hǎo！Hello, teacher!
2. 请 + Verb — Polite Requests
请 + Verb
Put 请 in front of a verb to turn an order into a polite request or invitation. It works with any verb: 进 (enter), 坐 (sit), 听 (listen), 说 (speak).
请进。qǐng jìn。Please come in.
请坐。qǐng zuò。Please sit down.
请听。qǐng tīng。Please listen.
请说。qǐng shuō。Please go ahead.
