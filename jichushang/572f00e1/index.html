📝 Notes
1. 要不 (yàobù) — otherwise; how about
要不
“要不” has two main uses: (1) Meaning “otherwise” — expressing what would happen if something were not done: (2) Making a suggestion — “how about...”:
这么晚了，赶快回家吧，要不家里会不放心的。zhè me wǎn le， gǎn kuài huí jiā ba， yào bù jiā lǐ huì bù fàng xīn de。Its late — hurry home, otherwise your family will worry.
赶快走，要不就赶不上火车了。gǎn kuài zǒu， yào bù jiù gǎn bù shàng huǒ chē le。Hurry up, or you will miss the train.
要不这样吧，我正好要买电脑，我陪你去一趟。yào bù zhè yàng ba， wǒ zhèng hǎo yào mǎi diàn nǎo， wǒ péi nǐ qù yī tàng。How about this — I happen to need to buy a computer, I will go with you.
要不咱们坐出租车去吧，打车快一点儿。yào bù zán men zuò chū zū chē qù ba， dǎ chē kuài yī diǎn ér。How about we take a taxi — it is faster.
2. 正好 (zhènghǎo) — just right; just in time; happen to
正好
“正好” means “just right”, “happen to”, or “coincidentally”. It describes a perfect fit in time, quantity, or circumstance.
我正好要买电脑，我陪你去一趟。wǒ zhèng hǎo yào mǎi diàn nǎo， wǒ péi nǐ qù yī tàng。I happen to need to buy a computer — I will go with you.
这件衣服大小正好。zhè jiàn yī fu dà xiǎo zhèng hǎo。This piece of clothing is just the right size.
你来得正好，我们正找你呢。nǐ lái de zhèng hǎo， wǒ men zhèng zhǎo nǐ ne。You have come just in time — we were looking for you.
3. 多 (duō) — indicating an approximate number
多
“多” is placed after numerals or measure words to indicate an approximate number. The position of “多” depends on the number structure.
他只想要个一千多的。tā zhǐ xiǎng yào gè yī qiān duō de。He only wants one that is a bit over 1,000 RMB.
教室里有二十多个学生。jiào shì lǐ yǒu èr shí duō gè xué sheng。There are more than 20 students in the classroom.
他等了三个多小时。tā děng le sān gè duō xiǎo shí。He waited for over three hours.
4. 听说 (tīngshuō) — I have heard that; someone told me
听说
“听说” literally means “hear-speak” — it introduces information obtained from others. The source may be specific or unknown. Functions like “I hear that...” or “apparently...”.
听说附近新开了个批发市场。tīng shuō fù jìn xīn kāi le gè pī fā shì chǎng。I hear there is a new wholesale market opened nearby.
听说你要去中国留学，真的吗？tīng shuō nǐ yào qù zhōng guó liú xué， zhēn de ma？I heard you are going to study in China — is it true?
听说这部电影很好看。tīng shuō zhè bù diàn yǐng hěn hǎo kàn。I have heard this movie is very good.
5. 真不用……了 (zhēn búyòng...le) — truly no need to
真不用……了
“真不用……了” means “truly there is no need to...”. It is used to politely persuade someone that an action is unnecessary.
你真不用去了，那儿我去过。nǐ zhēn bù yòng qù le， nà ér wǒ qù guò。You really dont need to go — I have been there.
你真不用客气。nǐ zhēn bù yòng kè qi。You really dont need to be so polite.
这真不用谢，举手之劳。zhè zhēn bù yòng xiè， jǔ shǒu zhī láo。There is really no need to thank me — it was nothing.
6. 不如 (bùrú) — not as good as; had better
不如
“不如” compares two things and indicates the latter is better. “A不如B + Adjective” means “A is not as (adjective) as B”. It can also suggest “it would be better to...”. 🔄 Clear ✓ Done 🖨 Print
东西真不如大商场的好。dōng xi zhēn bù rú dà shāng chǎng de hǎo。The things there really arent as good as those in big shopping malls.
他的汉语不如你好。tā de hàn yǔ bù rú nǐ hǎo。His Chinese is not as good as yours.
与其坐公交车，不如打车去。yǔ qí zuò gōng jiāo chē， bù rú dǎ chē qù。Rather than taking the bus, it is better to take a taxi.
