李钟文：你真聪明！你呢？

📝 Notes
1. 同屋 (tóngwū) — roommate
Literally “same room”. On Chinese campuses it means the person you share a dorm room with. In everyday speech 室友 (shìyǒu) is also common, but 同屋 sounds warmer and is what students usually call each other.
很高兴跟你同屋。hěn gāo xìng gēn nǐ tóng wū。Very glad to be your roommate.
我的同屋是美国人。wǒ de tóng wū shì měi guó rén。My roommate is American.
2. 请多多关照 — a set phrase for first meetings
请多多关照
Literally “please take care of me a lot”. It is a fixed polite formula used when introducing yourself to a group or a new class ー roughly “please be kind to me” / “I look forward to working with you”. You say it once, at the beginning; it is not a real request.
我叫望月智子，请多多关照。wǒ jiào wàng yuè zhì zi， qǐng duō duō guān zhào。I’m Mochizuki Tomoko — nice to meet you all.
3. 越来越 (yuèláiyuè) — more and more
越来越 + Adjective / Verb of change
Shows that a degree grows over time. It must be followed by an adjective or a verb that expresses change (喜欢, 懂, 会, 想). It cannot be followed by a simple action verb.
去法国的中国人越来越多。qù fǎ guó de zhōng guó rén yuè lái yuè duō。More and more Chinese people are going to France.
天气越来越凉快了。tiān qì yuè lái yuè liáng kuai le。The weather is getting cooler and cooler.
汉语越来越难。hàn yǔ yuè lái yuè nán。Chinese is getting harder and harder.
他越来越喜欢中国。tā yuè lái yuè xǐ huan zhōng guó。He likes China more and more.
✗我越来越吃饭。wǒ yuè lái yuè chī fàn。"Eat" is a plain action, not a change — say 我吃得越来越多 (I eat more and more).
4. 是……的 (shì…de) — emphasizing time, place, manner or agent
是 + [emphasized part] + Verb + 的
Used to highlight when, where, how, or who did something. The action must already have happened. The 的 goes at the end of the clause, not after the noun.
是公司派我来学习的。shì gōng sī pài wǒ lái xué xí de。It was the company that sent me here to study.
他是今年三月来北京的。tā shì jīn nián sān yuè lái běi jīng de。It was in March this year that he came to Beijing.
这本书是在网上买的。zhè běn shū shì zài wǎng shàng mǎi de。This book was bought online.
✗他是明天去北京。tā shì míng tiān qù běi jīng。The trip has not happened yet — 是……的 only works for past events. Say 他明天去北京。.
5. 先……然后…… (xiān…ránhòu…) — first…, then…
先 + V1，然后 + V2
Marks the order of two events. 先 sits before the first verb, 然后 introduces the second. 然后 can also be replaced by 再 or 接着.
我要先在这儿学习半年，然后在中国工作。wǒ yào xiān zài zhè ér xué xí bàn nián， rán hòu zài zhōng guó gōng zuò。I will first study here for half a year, then work in China.
先吃饭，然后去看电影。xiān chī fàn， rán hòu qù kàn diàn yǐng。Eat first, then go to the cinema.
你先问问他，然后再决定。nǐ xiān wèn wèn tā， rán hòu zài jué dìng。Ask him first, and then decide.
6. 一边……一边…… (yìbiān…yìbiān…) — doing two things at the same time
一边 + V1，一边 + V2
Two 一边 mark two actions going on simultaneously. Both verbs must be voluntary — something the subject can choose to do.
这个暑假，我要一边学汉语，一边学京剧。zhè ge shǔ jià， wǒ yào yī biān xué hàn yǔ， yī biān xué jīng jù。This summer I will study Chinese and Peking opera at the same time.
妈妈一边做饭，一边跟客人聊天儿。mā ma yī biān zuò fàn， yī biān gēn kè rén liáo tiān ér。Mom chats with the guest while cooking.
不要一边走路一边看手机。bù yào yī biān zǒu lù yī biān kàn shǒu jī。Don’t look at your phone while walking.
✗他一边受表扬，一边脸红了。tā yī biān shòu biǎo yáng， yī biān liǎn hóng le。Being praised and blushing are not voluntary actions — 一边…一边… does not fit here.
7. 为了 (wèile) — in order to; for the purpose of
为了 + purpose，+ action
Introduces a goal. It usually sits at the front of the first clause, and the second clause tells what was done to reach it. You can also state the action first and end with 是为了.
美国学生爱珍学汉语是为了学京剧。měi guó xué sheng ài zhēn xué hàn yǔ shì wèi le xué jīng jù。The American student Aizhen learns Chinese in order to study Peking opera.
为了学好汉语，她每天练习两个小时。wèi le xué hǎo hàn yǔ， tā měi tiān liàn xí liǎng gè xiǎo shí。In order to learn Chinese well, she practises two hours every day.
他每天跑步，是为了身体健康。tā měi tiān pǎo bù， shì wèi le shēn tǐ jiàn kāng。He runs every day for the sake of his health.
8. 一……也/都 + 不/没 — emphatic total negation
一 + Measure Word (+ Noun) + 也/都 + 不/没 + Verb
“Not even one …” — a much stronger way to say no. 一 is followed by a measure word; 也 or 都 comes before 不 / 没. Put 连 before 一 for extra emphasis.
他一个汉字也不认识。tā yī gè hàn zì yě bù rèn shi。He doesn’t know a single Chinese character.
教室里一个人都没有。jiào shì lǐ yī gè rén dōu méi yǒu。There isn’t a single person in the classroom.
我一口也没吃。wǒ yī kǒu yě méi chī。I didn’t eat even one bite.
