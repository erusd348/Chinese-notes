💬 Dialogue
课文 1
刘艳：这家饭馆儿的饭菜又好吃又便宜。这是菜单，你们喜欢吃什么？
飞龙：我一看汉语菜单就头疼，老师您先点吧。
刘艳：行，我先点一个吧。他们这儿有个拿手菜叫水煮牛肉。
飞龙：味儿怎么样？腻不腻？
刘艳：挺好吃的，一点儿也不腻，可是有点儿辣。你们吃得了吗？
李钟文：吃得了，我喜欢吃辣的，越辣我越喜欢。
望月：是吗？那再来个酸辣土豆丝，让你再多吃一点儿。
飞龙：甜味儿的菜有没有？我喜欢吃甜的。
刘艳：京酱肉丝是甜味儿的，是地道的北京风味。
李钟文：我吃过一个菜，叫“糖醋里脊”，又甜又酸，你一定喜欢。
课文 2
望月：菜够了，太多了咱们吃不了。再要个西红柿鸡蛋汤怎么样？
飞龙：好的。咱们喝点儿什么？
李钟文：啤酒！这么热的天，当然要喝冰啤酒了。
刘艳：我一喝啤酒就头疼、脸红，我和望月喝点儿果汁吧。
李钟文：行，不过，得先喝点儿啤酒，然后再喝果汁。
飞龙：对，一边喝中国啤酒，一边吃中国菜，一边说中国话，我们的汉语会越说越流利。
课文 3
星期五晚上，李钟文和同学一起去吃晚饭。他们去了学校旁边的一家饭馆儿。那儿又干净又凉快，环境很舒适。在学校附近的饭馆儿里，那儿的客人总是最多。留学生都喜欢在那儿吃饭，有时候去晚了，就没有座位了。
吃完以后，同学们觉得那儿的服务员又热情又周到，菜的味道也确实不错，就是有一点点咸。大家都说，以后下了课可以经常去那儿吃饭。
📝 Notes
1. 饭馆儿 (fànguǎnr) — small local restaurant
The 儿 suffix (erhua) is typical of northern speech — it makes a word sound small, casual and warm. A 饭馆儿 is a small neighbourhood place to eat; 饭店 is bigger and more formal (and in some cities means “hotel”). In the south people usually say 餐厅 or 饭店.
学校附近有家饭馆儿，又便宜又好吃。xué xiào fù jìn yǒu jiā fàn guǎn ér， yòu pián yi yòu hǎo chī。There is a small restaurant near campus — cheap and tasty.
这家饭馆儿的环境挺舒适的。zhè jiā fàn guǎn ér de huán jìng tǐng shū shì de。This restaurant has a quite comfortable atmosphere.
2. 拿手菜 (náshǒucài) / 地道 (dìdao) — specialty & authentic
拿手 means “skilful at”; a 拿手菜 is the dish a cook does best — the house specialty. 地道 (dìdao, note the neutral second syllable) means authentic, the real thing, and is the highest praise for regional food. Practical tip: asking for the 拿手菜 is the safest way to order somewhere you have never been.
水煮牛肉是这家饭馆儿的拿手菜。shuǐ zhǔ niú ròu shì zhè jiā fàn guǎn ér de ná shǒu cài。Beef in chilli sauce is this restaurant’s specialty.
他做的中国菜很地道。tā zuò de zhōng guó cài hěn dì dao。The Chinese food he cooks is really authentic.
3. 又……又…… (yòu…yòu…) — both … and …
又 + Adj / Verb₁ + 又 + Adj / Verb₂
Joins two qualities or actions that hold at the same time. Both slots take adjectives or verbs — and no degree adverb (很 / 非常 / 太) may appear inside the pattern, because 又 already carries the emphasis.
这家饭馆儿的饭菜又好吃又便宜。zhè jiā fàn guǎn ér de fàn cài yòu hǎo chī yòu pián yi。The food at this restaurant is both tasty and cheap.
她男朋友又高又帅。tā nán péng you yòu gāo yòu shuài。Her boyfriend is both tall and handsome.
他又想学汉语，又想学日语。tā yòu xiǎng xué hàn yǔ， yòu xiǎng xué rì yǔ。He wants to learn both Chinese and Japanese.
✗又很好吃又很便宜。yòu hěn hǎo chī yòu hěn pián yi。WRONG — no 很 inside 又…又… → 又好吃又便宜。.
4. 一……就…… (yī…jiù…) — as soon as
一 + Verb₁ + 就 + Verb₂
Two events follow each other with almost no gap. It also works as “once A, then B”. When both clauses share a subject, the subject before 就 is normally dropped: 他一回家就打开电视。
我一看汉语菜单就头疼。wǒ yī kàn hàn yǔ cài dān jiù tóu téng。My head hurts as soon as I look at a Chinese menu.
他一回家就打开电视。tā yī huí jiā jiù dǎ kāi diàn shì。He turns on the TV as soon as he gets home.
她一说汉语就脸红。tā yī shuō hàn yǔ jiù liǎn hóng。She blushes the moment she speaks Chinese.
5. 挺 + Adj (tǐng) — quite; pretty
挺 + Adj (+ 的)
A colloquial, softer 很. In speech it almost always travels with a final 的: 挺好吃的。Use it for opinions, not for things you would measure.
挺好吃的。tǐng hǎo chī de。It’s pretty tasty.
这个电影挺有意思的。zhè ge diàn yǐng tǐng yǒu yì si de。This film is quite interesting.
今天挺冷的，多穿一点儿吧。jīn tiān tǐng lěng de， duō chuān yī diǎn ér ba。It’s rather cold today — put on a bit more.
6. Verb + 得了 / 不了 — can / cannot manage
Verb + 得 / 不 + 了 (liǎo)
This 了 is read liǎo, not le. It asks whether an action is possible: 吃得了 = can manage to eat it, 吃不了 = cannot. The negative is far more common in real conversation. Do not confuse it with the result complement 记住了 (“have memorised it”).
这么多菜，我一个人吃不了。zhè me duō cài， wǒ yī gè rén chī bù liǎo。There’s so much food — I can’t finish it alone.
明天我有事，去不了了。míng tiān wǒ yǒu shì， qù bù liǎo le。I have something on tomorrow, so I can’t go after all.
你们吃得了吗？nǐ men chī de liǎo ma？Can you manage to eat it?
✗我吃不了太多菜了。wǒ chī bù liǎo tài duō cài le。Say 我吃不了这么多菜。— with 这么多 the object goes before the verb.
7. 越……越…… (yuè…yuè…) — the more … the more …
越 + Verb / Adj + 越 + Adj
The second half grows as the first grows. The two halves can share a subject (越学越有意思) or have different ones (越辣我越喜欢). With a verb + object, echo the verb: 越学越喜欢, not 越学汉语越喜欢.
我觉得汉语越学越有意思。wǒ jué de hàn yǔ yuè xué yuè yǒu yì si。The more I study Chinese, the more interesting it gets.
雨越下越大。yǔ yuè xià yuè dà。The rain is falling harder and harder.
越辣我越喜欢。yuè là wǒ yuè xǐ huan。The spicier it is, the more I like it.
8. 就是 (jiùshì) — the only thing is …
Positive clause，+ 就是 + drawback
A concession: you praise something first, then name the one small problem. It is weaker than 但是 — think “the only thing is …” rather than a real objection.
这件衣服很好看，就是有一点点贵。zhè jiàn yī fu hěn hǎo kàn， jiù shì yǒu yī diǎn diǎn guì。This looks great — the only thing is it’s a bit expensive.
这个手机不错，就是电池不太好。zhè ge shǒu jī bù cuò， jiù shì diàn chí bù tài hǎo。This phone is good; it’s just that the battery isn’t great.
他很聪明，就是不太努力。tā hěn cōng ming， jiù shì bù tài nǔ lì。He’s very smart — the only problem is he doesn’t work hard.
