📝 Notes
呢 for Exaggeration & Pride
The particle 呢 emphasises the fact while adding a slight sense of exaggeration and pride.
能游八百米呢néng yóu bā bǎi mǐ neI can swim 800 metres.
不怎么样 — ‘Not So Great’
不怎么样 expresses a negative evaluation. It has the same meaning as 不好 (not good).
踢得不怎么样tī de bù zěn me yàng(He) doesn’t play very well.
📚 Grammar
1. The state complement
Verb + 得 + Adj.
The complement placed after a verb and explaining the state of an action is called a state complement. The commonly used structure is: Verb + “得” + Adjective (or other elements), e.g.
你游泳游得怎么样？nǐ yóu yǒng yóu de zěn me yàng？How well do you swim?
我游得不错，能游八百米呢。wǒ yóu de bù cuò， néng yóu bā bǎi mǐ ne。I swim pretty well — I can swim 800 metres.
他球踢得真棒！tā qiú tī de zhēn bàng！He plays ball really well!
If the main verb of the predicate has an object, the verb should be repeated, e.g.
你汉话说得怎么样？nǐ hàn huà shuō de zěn me yàng？How is your spoken Chinese?
他说汉语说得很流利。tā shuō hàn yǔ shuō de hěn liú lì。He speaks Chinese very fluently.
2. A-not-A question (with state complement)
Verb + 得 + Adj. + 不 + Adj.
For sentences with a state complement, the affirmative-negative question form is: repeat verb + “得” + A-not-A, e.g.
你游泳游得好不好？nǐ yóu yǒng yóu de hǎo bù hǎo？Do you swim well or not?
他汉话说得流利不流利？tā hàn huà shuō de liú lì bù liú lì？Is his spoken Chinese fluent or not?
If the verb has an object, the question form is: Verb + object + Verb + “得” + A-not-A, e.g.
你汉语说得好不好？nǐ hàn yǔ shuō de hǎo bù hǎo？Do you speak Chinese well or not?
他汉字写得漂亮不漂亮？tā hàn zì xiě de piào liang bù piào liang？Does he write Chinese characters beautifully or not?
