📝 Notes
对了 — ‘By the Way / Oh, I Remember’
In spoken Chinese, 对了 is used when the speaker suddenly thinks of another topic or remembers something to add. It functions as an interjection.
📚 Grammar
1. Complement of potential
Verb + 得 / 不 + Complement
A complement of potential is formed with the structural particle “得” inserted between a verb and a complement of result or direction to indicate potentiality. The negative form is made by replacing “得” with “不”, e.g.
坐这儿不错，看得清楚，也听得清楚。zuò zhè ér bù cuò， kàn de qīng chu， yě tīng de qīng chu。Sitting here is good — you can see clearly and hear clearly too.
电影里的对话太快了，我可能听不懂。diàn yǐng lǐ de duì huà tài kuài le， wǒ kě néng tīng bù dǒng。The dialogue in the film is too fast; I probably won’t understand it.
六点钟太早了，我起不来。liù diǎn zhōng tài zǎo le， wǒ qǐ bù lái。Six o’clock is too early — I can’t get up that early.
The affirmative-negative question with a complement of potential is obtained by juxtaposing the affirmative form of the complement and the negative form of it, e.g.
这个问题回答得对不对？zhè ge wèn tí huí dá de duì bù duì？Is the answer to this question right or wrong?
If a verb has an object, the object is placed after the complement of potential. If the object is long and complex, it is normally placed at the beginning of the sentence, e.g.
我看不懂这本书。wǒ kàn bù dǒng zhè běn shū。I can’t understand this book.
有中文字幕，大概的意思你肯定看得懂。yǒu zhōng wén zì mù， dà gài de yì si nǐ kěn dìng kàn de dǒng。With Chinese subtitles you will certainly be able to follow the general meaning.
你要的那本书现在买不到。nǐ yào de nà běn shū xiàn zài mǎi bù dào。The book you want can’t be bought at the moment.
